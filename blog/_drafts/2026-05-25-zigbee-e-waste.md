I just got a new walking pad for my standing desk. It helps keep my newborn asleep if I'm in constant motion while I wear him in the Ergobaby carrier. Definite quality of life improvement!

Four years ago, I outfitted our home with motorized blackout shades. With the press of a button, a room can go dark. Definite quality of life improvement!

Oh, but, the remote for those blinds just bricked after being dropped on a hardwood floor. Now the blinds are stuck in the down position. There's no manual override. The remote is no longer sold by the company. Definite quality of life decrease!

---

With the purchase of the walking pad, which also can only be controlled via remote, I've come to the realization that all these devices, while providing real convenience, are all tech debt waiting for the margin call on that convenience. Ideally they'd build a better design such that the remote is unnecessary. I don't know when this stopped being a thing.

But now, to avoid the proverbial bank run on functionality, I need to reverse engineer every new "smart" device I purchase so I have backup control via a Zigbee dongle hooked up to a Raspberry Pi.
