There's a debate raging online about whether or not it's good or bad to read the code that LLMs are generating for you, before you ship it. Some think you should scrutinize every line and pick it apart until it resembles what they think they'd have written. For others, LLMs are revolutionizing how they think about authoring code, and the code itself is fading into the rearview mirror.

Personally, I try to read everything they generate for my projects, mostly so I can form a mental map of where eerything is. And sometimes I find little gems nestled in there. Things I wouldn't have known to use on my own. So, I don't think it's necessarily all bad to use LLMs or review their code.

Here's a list of them.

---

A **seam** is a piece of lingo that started cropping up a bunch in the LLM dialog, so eventually I asked it why. It turns out it's a real technical term: "place where you can alter behavior in your program without editing in that place". I use this constantly, not just with mocks for tests, but also with launch arguments and environment variables to reconfigure initial program state, alter conditional branching in implementations and conditionally compilating regions of source code. Reerence: [https://martinfowler.com/bliki/LegacySeam.html](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/LegacySeam.html).

**FNV-1a** a noncryptographic deterministic hash function, the Fowler-Noll-Vo hash, and the 1a is a variant of the original where it applies reverses the multiplication and XOR steps to produce better avalanche characteristics (another TIL, for the terminology, but I did previously konw the concept). This was used to dedupe certain inputs to maintain idempotency in a backing store. Reference: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fowler–Noll–Vo_hash_function#FNV-1a_hash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fowler–Noll–Vo_hash_function#FNV-1a_hash).

I could have sworn I had another one of these, but I can't find it right now. But, I'll add on to this list as I hit more of these.
