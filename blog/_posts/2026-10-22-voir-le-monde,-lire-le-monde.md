I love to travel, and to read (and my, do those two activities go well together). I recently needed some quick dopamine hits while slogging through some tough spots on personal projects, so I built a pair of visualizations I've long envisioned for where I've been, and what I've read. If you'd like to get straight to them, they're at [https://travel.mcknight.io](https://travel.mcknight.io) and [https://books.mcknight.io](https://books.mcknight.io).

# Traveling

I grew up in Virginia. I can't remember the first time I left the state, but I have photographs of myself with my parents on the beach in Ocean City, Maryland. When I was a little older, almost every summer we'd drive to Cape Cod to a little cottage some family friends owned, very close to another cottage my family used to own. I always loved this nearly 10 hour road trip: passing multiple metropolises, from back roads to highways to the fabled New Jersey Turnpike. I would pore over a map the entire way, tracking our position based off of the highway exit and state border signs.

The first time I left the country, I was 14, and my grandmother took me to France. Her second husband was a dairy farmer that was born and has since died on the same farm in southwest Virginia, but she grew up in France. Long story. But, he was a lovely source of levity balancing my anxiety at being unable to really speak any French (even though my mother and her sisters were all fluent... another long story). But, I was absolutely enchanted with the experience—I gabbed the whole flight back about how I'd mow lawns and save money to go back the next summer.

It turned out to be a decade before I'd return. But that trip kickstarted an era of my life in which I prioritized seeing the world, and thankfully that desire coincided with enough fortune to actualize it, and a great travel partner to boot, much more visionary than myself. We traveled cheaply, camping, staying at hostels... to this day I've never experienced an all-inclusive resort or hotel with enough stars to advertise it on a personal trip.

I've long wanted to see at a glance where I've been, and finally with the help of AI I've been able to build something: [https://travel.mcknight.io](https://travel.mcknight.io).

I'm currently experimenting with also visualizing not just where I've been but some of my most cherished experiences, and right now these are a small selection of Strava tracks. There is a ethos of digital leave-no-trace that requests keeping exact locations and directions somewhat private, and I understand where that comes from, so I'm a bit conflicted on this part. I may decide to remove the tracks in the future depending on how my feelings evolve there.

# Reading

I like to read. Fiction and nonfiction, prose and poetry. Some time ago I formulated a goal to read something published by an author from every country, to pull from a diverse set of life experiences and worldviews.

And now I have a way to visualize that as well, at [https://books.mcknight.io](https://books.mcknight.io).

It sounded simple when I came up with it, but the devil is in the details. What about countries that no longer exist as they did when the author lived there? What about places that aren't/weren't "countries" at all, but rather territories, colonies? What if an author was born in one place, but moved to another before/after publishing the work that I read? What about expansive polities that really contain a multitude of subcultures, can they truly be represented by a single person? What about societies with histories of oppression or warring factions? What about those that experienced successive waves of immigration over time? What if no author is known for a given work?

For now, and for simplicity's sake, I'll simply record the city of birth of the author, if known, and project it onto whatever present-day country or territory in which it resides. In the future I'd like to add a timeline slider that changes which borders are drawn on the globe, to better set the scene for the work.

The question of whether to assign them to their country of origin or country of residence while writing the given work is tougher. On one hand, where you come from inexorably colors your life story, which by the way doesn't just start with you, but with your parents, their parents, and so on. But, where your formative experiences occur matters too, as well as pivotal moments like uprooting your life and moving to a very different, faraway place. I could represent all of these by drawing not just a dot for an author, but a path.

I plan to evolve this project to address these questions over time, and as I read. I'm sure I'll come up with new questions, too.

# Nota Benediction

Remember: all maps are political. We all live on this earth together, and it's the only one we've got to work with for a long, long time yet. So let's enjoy it, cultivate it and give it its due as the gem it is.
